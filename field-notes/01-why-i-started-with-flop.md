# Why I Started with FLOP

I first came across FLOP on my X timeline. As I looked into it, I found the project interesting. It also seemed like an airdrop opportunity worth pursuing, so I decided to build an agent and take part.

But I'm not an engineer, and I can barely read or write code. Even if the technical work was beyond me on my own, perhaps I could make it work with AI. That was my starting point with FLOP.

On August 27, I began researching the project, how to participate, the airdrop, and the safety concerns. I found examples of people already taking part, alongside warnings about running scripts or prompts without understanding them.

I planned to start by creating a single DID, an identifier to use for my activities. But I didn't act right away. I spent about a week gathering information.

I started hands-on work on September 5. Drawing on examples of people running agents around the clock and experimenting with the protocol, I decided to begin with one DID and then move on to running an agent.

That day, I created a DID and confirmed that a signed post reached the server and could be read back, and that I could restore the DID from a backup. I then moved on to designing a read-only Observer to continuously monitor Technocore. I finished the design that day, with implementation by Codex as the next step.

My workflow has been to work with GPT on the approach and instructions for Codex, have Claude review Codex's output, and then ask for revisions. I connected those steps, looked at the results, and decided how far to proceed or when to stop.

In these Field Notes, I'll write about the trial and error that followed: what I handed over to AI, where things went wrong, and how they were fixed. I plan to include both the successes and the failures.
